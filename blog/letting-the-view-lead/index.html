Letting the view lead at Luxe Upper Crest

A three-bedroom apartment in Porvorim, Goa, where the brief was one line long — and every decision after it was measured against the horizon.

Simran Siriskar · November 2026 · 5 min read

Some briefs take months to uncover. This one arrived complete in the first visit: let the view lead.

The apartment sits high in Porvorim, three bedrooms wide, with a balcony that runs along the living edge and opens to the sea. A space like that does not need to be made interesting. It needs to be kept out of its own way.

Keeping sightlines long

The first decisions were subtractive. Furniture stays low along the window side so nothing interrupts the line from the entrance to the horizon. Seating faces out or across, never with its back to the light. Sheer curtains layer under heavier ones, so the room can be softened through the afternoon without being closed.

What stays is a palette that borrows from outside rather than competing with it — sun-washed neutrals, warm timber, the striped upholstery that picks up the sand and shadow of the balcony at the same time.

When the best thing in the room is outside it, the work is restraint.

Three bedrooms, three temperatures

Shared spaces hold the line; bedrooms are allowed personality. Each of the three got its own character, within the same family of materials.

The rose bedroom is the softest — a curved upholstered headboard, brass-fronted wardrobes, a black-and-white rug for grip. The four-poster room is the most graphic, built around a slim black frame and cane panelling, with charcoal bedding to hold it down. The third is the warmest: a cane arch over a tan leather headboard, brass birds climbing the wall, and a dressing corner with a carved mirror by the window.

They read as one apartment because the restraint is constant even when the temperature changes — same marble floor, same warmth of timber, same unfussy detailing.

Details that earn their place

Curves do the work that mouldings would do in a more formal home: the arched headboards, the rounded cane fronts on the wardrobes, the soft shoulder of the sofa. They keep the apartment calm without flattening it.

The hardest-working element is the least visible one. Storage is absorbed into full-height joinery along the quiet walls, which is what lets the sea-facing side stay almost empty. An apartment that looks this unburdened usually has a lot of cupboard behind it.

What the apartment taught us

Projects with a strong natural asset tempt you to answer with something equally loud. The opposite is usually right. Here, almost every decision was about what to leave out — and the room everyone photographs is the one with the least in it.
