Process

Five stages, clearly signed off

The most common question we are asked is not about style. It is about what actually happens, in what order, and how long it takes. Here is the whole sequence.

Simran Siriskar · November 2026 · 6 min read

An interior project goes wrong in predictable ways. Decisions get made twice. Work starts before the drawings are settled. Nobody is quite sure who approved the tile. The fix is not more meetings — it is a sequence everyone can see, where each stage closes before the next one opens.

We work in five. Each ends with a sign-off, and the next begins only on your approval.

01 · Foundation

Four weeks

Concept and theme development, layout options, zoning and the furniture plan, and a look-and-feel moodboard.

This is the stage that decides everything after it. We are working out how the space should be used before we work out how it should look: which wall the sofa wants, whether the dining area can breathe, where storage has to go so the rest of the house stays calm. The moodboard comes at the end of this stage, not the beginning — it summarises a direction we have already agreed on.

02 · Dimension

Three weeks

3D model, electrical drawings, plumbing layout.

The plan becomes a volume you can walk through. Seeing the space in 3D is where most clients catch the things a flat drawing hides — a passage that feels tight, a window that deserves more of the room. It is also where electrical and plumbing get fixed, which matters because these are the decisions that are expensive to revisit once work has begun. Every socket, switch and light point is located now.

Changes are cheap on paper, affordable in 3D and painful on site. The process is built to push decisions as early as they can honestly be made.

03 · Definition

Two to three weeks

Material and finish selections, kitchen drawings, wall treatments, false-ceiling drawings.

Surfaces get decided: stone, timber, paint, tile, hardware. We put physical samples in front of you wherever we can, in the light of the actual room — a finish that looks warm in a showroom can turn grey in a north-facing bedroom. The kitchen is drawn in detail here too, because it has more moving parts than any other room in the house.

04 · Detail

Two to three weeks

Detailed furniture drawings, upholstery and curtains, paint selection, lighting list.

Bespoke pieces are drawn to the millimetre for our in-house carpenters and painters — which is the stage where a wardrobe becomes the right wardrobe for that wall rather than a standard unit pushed against it. Fabrics, curtains and the full lighting list are locked at the same time, so nothing is being chosen in a hurry while execution is already running.

05 · Reveal

One week

Decor selection, compiled design document, staging, styling and the photoshoot.

The finishing layer: art, objects, textiles, the things that make a finished space feel collected rather than installed. You also receive the compiled design document — every drawing, finish and specification in one place, which is what you will want in two years when something needs matching or repairing.

Roughly twelve to fifteen weeks of design

Added up, the design stages run about twelve to fifteen weeks. Execution overlaps and extends beyond that, and its length depends on the scope — a styling project is weeks, a turnkey villa is months.

Two things keep that schedule honest. The first is the sign-off at the end of each stage: it is a real gate, and we do not start the next stage without it. The second is that we build a lot of what we draw, with our own team of carpenters and painters, so the gap between the drawing and the thing is shorter than it would otherwise be.
